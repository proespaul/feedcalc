How it works out the size

Voltage drop. Rule 8-102, page 89. Drop is figured on the connected load when you know it, otherwise on 80% of the breaker rating. The limit is 3% on a branch circuit or feeder and 5% from the service to the point of use.

The drop itself. Table D3, page 784: VD = K x f x I x L / 1000, with K in ohms per circuit kilometre at 75 °C, f = 2 single phase and DC, f = 1.73 three phase line to line, and L the one-way length in metres. Note 3 multiplies K by 0.95 at 60 °C and 1.05 at 90 °C.

Ampacity. Rule 4-004, page 76: Table 2 for copper and Table 4 for aluminum, one to three conductors in a raceway or cable at 30 °C. Table 5A corrects for ambient above 30 °C, Table 5C for more than three current-carrying conductors. Rule 4-006, page 78, holds the size to the column your terminations are marked for: 60 °C when unmarked at 100 A or less, 75 °C above that.

Continuous load. Rule 8-104: a load running three hours or more is sized at 125%, which is the same as holding it to 80% of the conductor and the device.

Breaker. Rule 14-104, page 159: the device does not exceed the conductor ampacity, except for the Table 13 round-up to the next standard size up to 800 A. Small conductors are capped at 15 A for #14 copper, 20 A for #12 copper, 30 A for #10 copper, 15 A for #12 aluminum and 25 A for #10 aluminum.

Connectors. On a cable run each end is picked on its own, so a feeder can leave an indoor panel on a dry connector and land in an outdoor disconnect on a weatherproof teck gland. The weatherproof pick is made on the cable diameter against the range printed on the connector itself, which is why it can tell you when a cable falls between two stocked sizes. Cable diameters are typical figures and vary by make, so check the range against the reel before you order.

Bonding. Rule 10-616 with Table 16: the bond is sized on the breaker rating, or on the conductor ampacity where the conductors were upsized for voltage drop, and never has to be larger than the circuit conductors. A bonding means built into the cable counts (10-616 7), and so does a metal raceway that is allowed to serve as the bond (10-616 6), which is why ACWU90 and Teck show no separate bond line.

Two runs, one job. The subpanel feeder is sized completely on its own: its own supply, length, cable, connectors and bonding. The two runs meet at the price, where the material from both is added up before the markup tier is chosen, so a job that clears a tier boundary gets the lower rate on all of it.

Check the tables once. The ampacity tables and K values are built into the app so it works with no signal. Read them against your own book the first time you use it, and tell me if a cell disagrees.

Not covered here. Alberta STANDATA amendments, derating for conduit fill, harmonic loading on a shared neutral, motor starting drop, and parallel runs. Those are still a look in the book.
